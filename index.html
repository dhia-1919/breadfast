Appointments and onboarding

A new rider books a kit-issuance slot at their warehouse from a one-tap link. The warehouse sees capacity per slot, the POC gets a digest every evening, and cancelling a service for a day is one action, not fourteen.

Why it matters

No-shows are the most expensive minutes in a warehouse: a counter is staffed, a kit is prepared, a rider is not earning. Reminders the evening before and a POC who knows exactly who is coming are the two cheapest levers on that number, and both run without anyone remembering to do them.

2

Gear assignment at the counter

The kit is defined per role, vehicle and warehouse. Each item is scanned to the rider, the rider signs on the tablet, and stock moves the instant the receipt is confirmed. Walk through an issuance and watch the warehouse inventory change.

Reorder point per item is set per warehouse. Falling below it raises an exception in the control tower and drafts a replenishment request automatically.

Why it matters

The signature is the point. Every future deduction, exchange or clearance dispute resolves against a timestamped receipt the rider acknowledged, in their language, with the item barcodes on it. No receipt, no argument.

3

Exchanges and replacements

Not every replacement is the same. The system routes each request to one of four exchange types, and the type decides who pays, who approves and where the old item goes. Answer the questions the counter staff would answer.

New exchange / Youssef Abdallah · R-0871 · insulated bag

Answer the first question

The exchange type, charge, approval requirement and destination of the returned item will resolve here.

Lifecycle exchangeItem reached end of life. No charge. Old item to disposal or reuse pool.
Size exchangeWrong fit inside the fit window. No charge. Old item back to stock.
Damage or lossChargeable at replacement value. Evidence required. Feeds a deduction.
Exception exchangeOut of policy. Manager approval required. Charge decided case by case.
Why it matters

When every replacement is just "gave him a new bag", the loss rate is invisible. Typed exchanges make the difference between wear-and-tear and negligence measurable per warehouse, which is the number procurement and HR both want and neither has.

    Deductions and penalties, governed

    Your riders are employees, so a deduction is a payroll event with legal weight, not an invoice line. Nothing here is automatic except the evidence. Every deduction runs through the same eight steps, and the rider can contest it from their phone before payroll ever sees it.

    1 · Rule triggersNo-show, loss, damage, non-compliance, overdue return
    2 · Evidence attachedReceipt, photo score, gate log, appointment record
    3 · Rider notifiedIn their language, with the evidence
    4 · Dispute windowConfigurable, default 7 days
    5 · Rider contestsComment and photo from the app
    6 · Manager decidesApprove, reduce or waive. Always a human.
    7 · Payroll exportApproved lines only, per cycle

    7

    Offboarding and clearance

    An exit in the HR system books a return appointment automatically. At the counter each item is checked in and graded, the outstanding balance is computed on the spot, and HR and payroll receive a clearance certificate. Grade the items and watch the balance move.

    Clearance / Hany Fathy · R-0512 · Sheikh Zayed · last day 19 Sept 2026Return appointment kept
    Outstanding balance EGP 0 All items returned in acceptable condition.
    Back to stockGood condition, reissued as new
    Reusable poolWorn but serviceable, second-hand inventory kept apart
    DisposeEnd of life, counted for shredding and waste cost
    TransferMoved to a warehouse that needs it
    MissingNot returned, charged at replacement value
    Why it matters

    The last day is when gear disappears. A return appointment that books itself and a balance HR sees before the final salary run is the difference between recovering a bag and writing it off. The five outcomes also give procurement the reuse and disposal numbers it currently guesses.
